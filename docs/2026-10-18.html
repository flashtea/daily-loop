Trump waived nearly all of the Graham Act's Russia sanctions and tariffs two days before they were due to take effect

The executive order, signed on 16 October, formally raises duties on Russian goods by 500% and on the five largest buyers of Russian oil and gas by 100%, then waives those duties and the Act's sanctions provisions (sections 102 through 111 and 117) with no end date, certifying to Congress that the waiver "is in the national interests of the United States". The Act, which Trump signed on 18 September after passing the Senate 86–11, required implementation by 18 October; sanctions imposed under earlier executive orders stay in force, and the order follows a 9 October plan to buy Russian diesel under a Treasury licence that runs to 7 April.

why it mattersThe one statutory lever that would have forced tariffs on China and India for buying Russian energy is suspended at presidential discretion, so plan for Russian crude to keep flowing and treat congressional sanctions acts as signals rather than constraints.

UKMTO counted at least 12 ships attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz in the week to 16 October, and said most attacked vessels were no longer reporting to it

Of the twelve, one sank (the container ship GFS Pride), two were disabled and three were damaged; 125 ships left and 129 entered the strait over the seven days, and CENTCOM said 400 ships and 100 million barrels of oil moved through under the US-coordinated escort system. On 17 October the IRGC said a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels hit a mine on an "unauthorised" route, which CENTCOM disputed, and UKMTO reported a tanker hit by a rocket-propelled grenade fired from a small boat 78 nautical miles northeast of Jubail.

why it mattersTransits are running at a fraction of normal under escort and the attack count is rising rather than falling, so keep $100-plus oil in your base case through the US midterms rather than treating last week's spike as a peak.

OpenCode said attackers took stored provider API keys from about 1.4% of its Console users through a legacy system it was decommissioning

The company behind the open-source coding agent said on 17 October that unauthorised access to a system being retired exposed OpenAI, Anthropic and Google keys saved in user workspaces, that everyone affected had been emailed, and that the breach was tied to a "large scale distillation attack"; it is working with the three providers and called the incident ongoing. Affected users reported usage alerts and charges of several hundred dollars on keys they had used only in OpenCode before the notice arrived.

why it mattersIf you ever pasted a provider key into OpenCode Console, revoke it today and check usage; more generally, give agent tools their own scoped, spend-capped keys rather than the account's main one.

OpenCode

Anthropic reported that juniors fell from 27% to 9% of its technical hires as its agents' working hours overtook its employees'

The company's economics team published internal data on 16 October: agent hours passed human hours company-wide in March 2026 and reached six times their handlers' hours by August, commits per engineer are about five times the level of six months earlier, the junior share of technical staff fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the median employee spends 20% more time in meetings than a year ago. The authors found no difference in how often agent-written and human-written code was later reverted.

why it mattersThis is one lab's workflow roughly a year ahead of most teams: expect fewer junior roles, hiring that grades judgment and a track record with agents over domain depth, and your own review time to become the bottleneck.

Anthropic said Claude traced the NIST elliptic-curve seeds to a 1990s C-library random generator, settling a decades-old question about whether the curves were backdoored

An internal research model noticed that the top bit of 20 words was zero in four of the seeds, odds of about one in 5,810 by chance, guessed the lrand48 generator seeded with a Unix timestamp, and recovered generator states by lattice reduction that match srand48 initialisations between May 1997 and April 1998 for P-192 through P-521 and five ANSI X9.62 example curves. Ten Anthropic authors published reproduction code on 16 October and say the result is not a weakness in ECDSA; the B-163 seed and most base points remain unexplained, and the $36,864 bounty for the seeds goes to the Internet Security Research Group.

why it mattersA model did, in one investigation, a piece of cryptanalytic archaeology the field had left open since 1999, so count frontier models as research collaborators rather than assistants and expect the "backdoored NIST curves" argument to fade from cryptography debates.

The General Conference on Weights and Measures voted to stop inserting leap seconds into UTC from 20 May 2027

Resolution 5 of the 28th CGPM, adopted in Versailles on 15 October, makes UTC continuous from that date and lets the difference between UTC and Earth-rotation time UT1 grow to 3,600 seconds before any correction, enough for several centuries; the 2022 conference had set 2035 as the deadline, and the date was brought forward because a faster-spinning Earth made a never-tried negative leap second likely.

why it mattersAfter 20 May 2027 no leap second will ever be inserted again, so the leap-second and clock-smearing code paths in your systems become dead weight to remove rather than maintain.
