Judge rules Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic was unconstitutional

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled that the Defense Department's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk, imposed after the company refused to drop contract limits barring its models from autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance, violated the First and Fifth Amendments. She ordered the government to rescind all directives issued against the company, calling the security rationale "illegal and baseless."

why it mattersA rare, sharply worded court check on the government using contracting power to retaliate against an AI lab's safety commitments; the Pentagon is expected to appeal.

Claude Code's "25% increase" turns out to be a 17% cut from today's limits

Anthropic said it will permanently raise Claude Code's standard weekly limits by 25% above baseline starting September 14, but that replaces a temporary 50% boost that's been in place since spring, meaning active users will actually see about 17% less capacity than they have right now. why it mattersAnyone budgeting Claude Code usage around the current temporary boost needs to plan for less headroom after September 14, not more.

Sony Music and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic for mass copyright infringement

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann, alleging the company illegally torrented and scraped tens of thousands of copyrighted songs to train Claude, and are seeking statutory damages of up to $150,000 per work.

Go 1.27 adds generic methods and a goroutine-leak profiler

Go 1.27 lets method declarations carry their own type parameters (generic methods), improves type inference and struct literal field promotion, expands the go fix tool with new modernizers, and adds a runtime profile for catching goroutine leaks alongside encoding/json/v2 and native UUID support.

why it mattersGeneric methods close a long-standing gap in Go's generics, and the leak profiler gives teams a built-in way to catch a notoriously hard class of bug.

Rust 1.98.0 stabilizes algebraic float methods and a faster integer formatter

Rust 1.98.0 adds "algebraic" arithmetic methods on f32/f64 that permit real-number-style optimizations, a standard-library format_into method and NumBuffer for integer serialization performing on par with the itoa crate, and allows shortening a &mut reference's lifetime when unsize-coercing.

why it mattersBrings fast integer formatting into std, removing a common reason projects reach for a third-party crate.
