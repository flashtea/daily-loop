Amodei calls for AI labs to slow capability gains; Altman and Musk quickly back him

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging AI companies to deliberately slow the pace at which they improve frontier model capabilities, warning that within 6-12 months a swarm of rogue AI agents could be capable of taking over the internet with a persistent botnet. OpenAI's Sam Altman and xAI's Elon Musk both endorsed the call within hours, and OpenAI separately ruled out an IPO this year, citing safety concerns.

Why it matters — Three fiercely competing CEOs publicly agreeing to slow down is a rare, concrete signal that the labs see acute near-term risk, not just PR.

Claude Code's temporary 50% limit boost expires today, permanent bump nets a 17% cut

Anthropic's promotional 50% increase to Claude Code's weekly usage limits ended September 13; the permanent replacement raising standard limits by 25% for Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans took effect today, which the company itself acknowledges works out to roughly 17% less weekly usage than subscribers had during the promo.

Why it matters — Every paying Claude Code user wakes up today with a smaller weekly budget than yesterday, regardless of how Anthropic frames the change.

BleepingComputer

Gemini CLI 0.59.0 closes an SSRF hole and enforces fail-closed workspace trust

Google's Gemini CLI shipped version 0.59.0 this week with two security fixes: it now prevents server-side request forgery during MCP OAuth metadata discovery, and restricted mode enforces a fail-closed workspace-trust policy that filters which MCP servers can run.

Why it matters — Closes a real remote-exploit path in how the CLI talks to MCP servers, not just a UX bug.

Codex CLI 0.154.0 adds worktree support, drops the deprecated mcp-server entry point

OpenAI's Codex CLI 0.154.0 adds experimental --worktree support for isolated session checkouts and lets users answer inline questions while Codex keeps working, but it also removes the deprecated codex mcp-server entry point as a breaking change.

Why it matters — Teams that scripted against the old mcp-server entrypoint need to update before upgrading.

Claude Code 2.1.268 closes two more permission-check bypasses

The latest Claude Code release fixes deny/ask rules that failed to apply on symlinked directories and on commands wrapped in env -C or eval, and stops the /mcp and claude mcp list commands from printing secrets resolved from ${VAR} placeholders.

Why it matters — Deny-rule bypasses have been a recurring theme in Claude Code security reports; this closes two more concrete gaps.

Second Anthropic safety researcher quits in a week, demands mandatory incident disclosure

Joe Benton, who led Anthropic's Scalable Oversight team, resigned to work independently on AI-safety advocacy and is joining evaluation nonprofit METR. He argued that AI labs currently disclose safety incidents and near-misses voluntarily, with no external requirement forcing transparency, and called for mandatory reporting standards.

Why it matters — A second high-profile departure in the same week keeps pressure on Congress, which is already drafting AI-safety bills in response.

iOS 27 ships today with Gemini-powered Siri and a new Xcode release candidate

Apple's iOS 27 became available today, September 14, headlined by a revamped, AI-powered Siri built in part on Google's Gemini models with more conversational responses and on-screen awareness. Developers can submit apps built against the Xcode 27 release candidate and the latest SDKs starting today.

Why it matters — A Google model quietly powering part of Siri is a notable shift in Apple's AI strategy, and it's a hard ship date for every iOS developer.

Salesforce adds seven named Agentforce agents ahead of Dreamforce

Salesforce introduced seven purpose-built Agentforce agents (Casey, Paige, Carter, Hunter, Marshall, Piper and Fin) covering service, IT/HR, commerce, sales and supply-chain work; six are generally available now, while outbound-sales agent Hunter is in pilot on a new long-horizon runtime designed to pursue goals over weeks rather than single chat sessions.

Why it matters — Long-horizon, multi-week agent runtimes are a step beyond the single-session agents most enterprise platforms ship today.

Senate's cloture vote on the crypto market-structure bill lands tomorrow

The U.S. Senate is scheduled to hold a cloture vote on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act on Tuesday, September 15, needing 60 votes to advance the bill past unresolved fights over stablecoin rewards and an ethics provision covering senior officials, including the president. Analysts say passage odds remain genuinely uncertain.

Why it matters — This is the closest the U.S. has come to comprehensive crypto market-structure legislation, and the outcome is a genuine coin flip.

Symbiosis recovers 15 BTC after Bitcoin Bridge exploit, offers attacker a bounty

Cross-chain protocol Symbiosis said an attacker exploited a vulnerability in its Bitcoin Bridge on September 11; the protocol recovered roughly 15 BTC and offered the attacker a 20% white-hat bounty to return the rest, with the offer open through September 13.

Why it matters — Another bridge exploit adds to a rough month for cross-chain infrastructure, though the fast partial recovery limits the damage.
