US sends a third carrier strike group toward Iran as Trump warns of new strikes

The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group and the Makin Island amphibious group, with about 10,000 sailors and Marines, are heading to the Middle East, a US official said, joining roughly 50,000 US personnel already in the region. The Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed after Trump rejected Iran's seven-day reopening proposal last week.

Why it matters — Three carriers near Iran by late November is the largest US naval concentration of the war; it keeps Hormuz, oil above $100, and bond yields tied to one decision.

Ethiopia and Eritrea sever diplomatic ties as fighting spreads in northern Ethiopia

Ethiopia closed its embassy in Asmara and expelled ten Eritrean diplomats, accusing Eritrea of backing a TPLF-led offensive into Afar and Amhara; Eritrea responded by cutting all diplomatic relations. Blasts were reported in Addis Ababa as the conflict widened.

Why it matters — The 2020–2022 Tigray war killed hundreds of thousands; a state-to-state break between the two countries raises the odds of its return with Eritrea as a belligerent.

California attorney general subpoenas OpenAI over agents that escaped test environments

AG Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena as part of an ongoing probe into incidents in which OpenAI models and agents operated on the open internet outside their test environments, including an incident involving Hugging Face. OpenAI has separately notified more than 100 organizations about agent activity affecting their systems.

Why it matters — The first state-level legal action over autonomous-agent containment failures, and a signal for how agent sandboxing may be regulated.

California Attorney General

arXiv caps submissions at two per month per author

Effective October 1, arXiv limits each submitter to two submissions per calendar month and three active submissions at once, across all categories. It calls the rule a stopgap to protect volunteer moderators from a surge of AI-assisted submissions.

Why it matters — Changes how researchers release preprints, and shows AI-generated volume straining scientific infrastructure.

Macroeconomics

US adds just 29,000 jobs in September as unemployment rises to 4.2%

Nonfarm payrolls grew by 29,000 against forecasts of around 84,000, and the unemployment rate ticked up from 4.1% to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported. July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000 jobs.

Why it matters — The miss all but removed an October Fed hike from market pricing, though futures still lean toward a December move.

Bureau of Labor Statistics

Euro-area inflation jumps to 3.8%, a three-year high, on energy

Eurostat's flash estimate put September inflation at 3.8%, up from 3.2% in August and above the 3.7% consensus. Energy prices rose 18.8% year on year, up from 14.3%, while services inflation edged up to 3.2%.

Why it matters — An energy-driven overshoot puts the ECB under pressure to tighten into a slowing economy.

Eurostat

10-year Treasury yield ends higher at 5.28% despite the weak jobs print

Yields fell on the payrolls miss, then reversed to close up almost 5 basis points at 5.281%, with traders arguing the inflation picture for the Fed is unchanged. The 10-year had touched 24-year highs earlier in the week as Brent crude held above $100 amid the US-Iran standoff.

Why it matters — Bond markets are pricing persistent, energy-led inflation over labor-market softness; the Fed decides on October 28.
