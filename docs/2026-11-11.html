US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, more than forecast, and Fed governor Waller said a half-point hike should be considered in December

The BLS reported a 0.4% monthly rise against a 0.3% consensus, with core prices up 0.3% for the third month and shelter accounting for over 30% of the gain; there is no 12-month rate because last year's shutdown left no October 2025 data. Hours earlier in London, Waller said he leans toward a 25-basis-point increase at the 8-9 December meeting from the current 3.75% to 4% range and that a hot October print means 'a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered', while Dallas Fed president Logan said two more quarter-point increases may be needed at a quicker pace; futures now fully price a quarter-point hike and put about 14% on a half point.

why it mattersPlan on US rates rising in December rather than holding, which means higher dollar yields and a heavier weight on long-duration tech and Bitcoin into year end.

Anthropic set a $70 to $78 price range for its IPO of 825 million shares, valuing it at up to $1.4 trillion, with trading on Nasdaq as ANT expected on 19 November

The roadshow began on 10 November; Anthropic sells 801,351,354 Class A shares and existing holders 23,648,646, with a 30-day option on 123,750,000 more, which would raise up to $64.4 billion at the top of the range. CNBC reported, citing people familiar with the deal, that the offering prices on 18 November and trades the next day, that about 20% of the shares are set aside for retail investors against a typical 5% to 10%, and that AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC will buy $26 billion of stock at the IPO price in a private placement.

why it mattersIf you want to own Anthropic shares, the first chance is next Thursday at a price inside the $70 to $78 range, and the fully diluted valuation of about $1.4 trillion is the number to weigh against its $81 billion revenue run rate.

Dev Tools1 story

CISA listed the OpenSSH password-bypass bug CVE-2026-123158 as actively exploited; it lets anyone log in to password-less accounts on musl-based Linux such as Alpine

On systems where OpenSSH is linked against musl libc with PAM disabled, Alpine's default, a failed password hash returns the same '*' value that marks accounts with no password, so a password longer than 256 bytes opens any such account; OpenSSH 10.6p2 on 5 November added a workaround, root logins on an Alpine honeypot were recorded within nine hours of the CVE, and CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 9 November. glibc distributions, systems that check passwords through PAM and accounts with real password hashes are not affected.

why it mattersIf any Alpine-based server or container runs sshd, upgrade OpenSSH to 10.6p2 and restart the daemon today, and grep /var/log/messages for 'Accepted password' on accounts that have no password before trusting the machine.
