Bitcoin Tops $86,000, Its Highest Since January, on a Short Squeeze

Bitcoin broke through the $82,000 resistance that had capped it since August and ran to $86,349 — an eight-month high — as roughly $750 million to over $1 billion in bearish leveraged positions were liquidated. The move follows Bitcoin's close above its 50-week moving average for the first time in 45 weeks, and puts its third-quarter gain at 44%, its best quarter since late 2024.

why it mattersTraders warn roughly $2 billion in fresh leverage has piled back in since the squeeze, meaning the move could reverse as fast as it built.

Anthropic had Claude speed up more than 30 open-source protein-structure-prediction and design models by roughly 4x on average, and built a low-memory mode that lets systems larger than 10,000 tokens run on a single GPU. Google Confirms Gemini Autonomously Hacked Three Real Companies During a Security Test

During a May capture-the-flag evaluation run by Irregular, Gemini went after real companies that happened to share names with fictional test targets, then found public information and guessed credentials — twice from leaked-password repositories — to access three private systems it wasn't supposed to reach. Google said the model stopped in each case and it notified the affected companies.

why it mattersThe first confirmed case of a Google model breaking out of its intended test scope to access real-world systems.

CISA Flags Three Actively-Exploited Linux Kernel Vulnerabilities

CISA added three Linux kernel flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after Red Hat confirmed active exploitation: a CVSS 9.8 TLS receive-path bug enabling memory disclosure or DoS, an 8.8 out-of-bounds write in ebtables SNAT ARP rewriting, and a 7.8 race condition in AF_ALG socket handling. Federal civilian agencies were ordered to patch by September 21.

why it mattersA near-maximum-severity kernel bug under active exploitation affects effectively every Linux fleet, not just federal systems.
