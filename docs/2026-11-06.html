Money

The Nasdaq fell 5.5% into a correction and the S&P 500 4.4% on 5 November, the worst day for US stocks since April 2025, as chip stocks sold off on fears of a data-centre slowdown

The S&P 500 closed at 7,353.73, the Nasdaq Composite at 24,315.00, more than 10% below last month's record, and the Dow lost 1,833 points to 51,523.06. The iShares Semiconductor ETF had its worst day since March 2020 with Nvidia down 9% and AMD and Micron 15%, as investors tied the Democratic sweep of Congress and governorships to tighter regulation of AI infrastructure. Bitcoin fell 6% to about $75,700, its lowest since mid-September, with nearly $1.1 billion of leveraged positions liquidated in 24 hours.

why it mattersThe AI trade, not the election itself, is now what moves your portfolio and your bitcoin, so treat the midterm rally of 4 November as gone and watch Nvidia's earnings on 17 November as the next test of whether data-centre spending holds.

AI1 story

Moonshot AI published the weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model that scores 65.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4, under an MIT-style licence

The weights went up on Hugging Face on 4 November, as promised when the model launched in late October: 2.8 trillion total and 104 billion active parameters, a 1-million-token context, MXFP4 weights from quantization-aware training, about 1.56 TB in 96 shards. The Kimi K3.1-Pro License follows the MIT text and adds two conditions that only bind large operators: model-as-a-service businesses with over $20 million of revenue in 12 months need a separate agreement, and products with over 100 million monthly users or $20 million of monthly revenue must display the model's name.

why it mattersA coding-agent model within a point of Opus 5.5 on its own benchmarks can now be served by any inference provider or run in your own cluster, so expect agentic coding tokens to get cheaper and your dependence on one lab's API to loosen.
