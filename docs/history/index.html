Part I — Before civilization

  1. Ch. 1
    Why study the whole arc Five threads from the first stone tool to the first software agent

    History read as a sequence of kings is mostly noise. Read as the story of how humans captured energy, built tools, stored information, organized money, and projected force, it becomes a single compounding process with a few rules that still hold. This chapter sets out those five threads and the lens this series will use.

    03 Oct
  2. Ch. 2
    The first tools Oldowan choppers, Acheulean hand-axes, and a million years of one design

    Around 2.6 million years ago hominins in East Africa learned to strike one stone against another and get a sharp edge. The trick, controlled fracture, gave them access to meat and marrow their teeth could not reach, and that calorie surplus is the first move in the energy thread. The second design, the hand-axe, then stayed essentially the same for over a million years, which tells us as much about early minds as the tool itself.

    04 Oct
  3. Ch. 3
    Fire Cooked food, the bigger brain, the longer day, and the first energy technology

    Stone tools let hominins borrow calories from carcasses. Fire let them borrow calories from chemistry itself, by doing half of digestion outside the body. Controlled fire is the first time humans captured an energy source other than their own muscles, and the consequences ran through every one of this series' threads: a smaller gut and larger brain, a day that did not end at dusk, a hearth that made a group, and eventually the kilns and furnaces that turn clay and ore into civilization.
