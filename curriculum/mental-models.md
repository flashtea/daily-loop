# Mental Models — the syllabus

One lesson per day, in this order. This is the "latticework of mental models"
Charlie Munger described: the big ideas from the big disciplines, learned well
enough that they hang together and get used. The loop writes lesson N+1 where N
is the number of files already in `lessons/mental-models/`. Entries are
numbered continuously across parts; each line is `N. **Title** — scope`.

Sequencing principle: tools for thinking first, then the psychology of
misjudgment (Munger's own list, one tendency per day), then the hard sciences
and mathematics that supply the most general models, then economics, systems,
engineering, and finally decision-making under uncertainty where everything is
combined. When fewer than 30 entries remain unwritten, append a new part in the
same style (ideas from law, military strategy, philosophy, computer science,
and more case studies of models in combination).

## Part I — Tools for thinking

1. **The latticework** — why isolated facts don't stick, Munger's "man with a hammer", and the goal of this whole series
2. **The map is not the territory** — all models are wrong, some are useful; when to distrust the map
3. **Circle of competence** — knowing the edge of what you know, and what to do at the edge
4. **Inversion** — "invert, always invert": solving problems backward, avoiding stupidity over seeking brilliance
5. **First-principles reasoning** — reasoning from fundamentals instead of by analogy; when analogy is actually the better tool
6. **Second-order thinking** — "and then what?"; consequences of consequences
7. **Probabilistic thinking** — thinking in bets, base rates, and the difference between a good decision and a good outcome
8. **Bayesian updating** — priors, evidence, and changing your mind by the right amount
9. **Expected value** — magnitude times probability; why rare, large outcomes dominate
10. **Margin of safety** — building slack into engineering, investing, and plans
11. **Occam's razor** — simpler explanations and the cost of extra assumptions
12. **Hanlon's razor** — never attribute to malice what stupidity or incentives explain
13. **Thought experiments** — Galileo's ship, Einstein's elevator, and running cheap experiments in your head
14. **Opportunity cost** — the real cost of anything is what you give up
15. **The two-list method and focus** — Buffett's 25/5 story, saying no, and attention as the scarce resource
16. **Checklists** — why experts need them most (Gawande, aviation, surgery, Munger's investing checklist)
17. **Falsifiability** — Popper, what a real test looks like, and why "unfalsifiable" is a warning sign
18. **Steelmanning and the Ideological Turing Test** — arguing the other side well enough to pass as them
19. **Chesterton's fence** — understand why something exists before removing it
20. **Reversibility and one-way doors** — Bezos's type 1 / type 2 decisions; speed versus caution
